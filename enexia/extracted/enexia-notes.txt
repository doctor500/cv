Enexia’s Core Business • Renewable energy aggregator, not a simple power trader. o Traders: buy low, sell high, close positions for profit. o Aggregators: forecast renewable output, trade or sell electricity, act as intermediary between generators and consumers. • Mission: stabilize the power grid and maintain frequency, while also optimizing for profit.  How the Japanese Power System Works • Grid frequency: 50 Hz (east), 60 Hz (west). • Balance is critical — if demand > supply, frequency falls; if supply > demand, frequency rises. Large deviations risk blackouts. • Every generator (e.g., JERA, Summit Energy) must forecast tomorrow’s supply and demand for their assets and contracts, 24/7/365. • A central organization aggregates forecasts into the regional base forecast. • Major utilities (TEPCO, Kansai Electric, etc.) adjust by turning plants on/off if forecasts miss.  Role of a Renewable Energy Aggregator • Many renewable owners cannot forecast output. Aggregators forecast on their behalf for a fee. • Core tasks: o Forecast generation from assigned plants. o Submit forecasts 24/7. o Manage imbalances (deviation between forecast and actual generation). • Imbalance settlement: o If generation > forecast → utilities buy surplus. o If generation < forecast → utilities supply deficit. o Settlement price is volatile, set every 30 minutes, and unknown until real time. • Trading aspect: o By biasing forecasts (slightly higher/lower), aggregators can take positions like in finance. o Example: if imbalance price is expected high, forecast lower to sell high; if low, forecast higher to buy cheap. o Core principle remains: support the grid, but optimize to protect profits and generate returns. • Current revenues: fees from forecasting exceed trading profits, but trading returns are growing. 
 Forecasting & Market Challenges • Wind vs. Solar o Our portfolio is currently more wind than solar (rare in Japan, due to Sumitomo-led projects). o Wind forecasting is harder and less accurate (±20% error). o Solar forecasts are somewhat better (10–15% error). o These errors, multiplied by volatile imbalance prices, drive significant P&L swings. • Price Forecasting o Imbalance price forecasting is extremely challenging. o Accuracy of 55–60% is considered strong, and even modest advantage compounded over time yields returns. • Weather Dependence o Forecast accuracy hinges on external weather forecasts. o With actual weather data, generation could be predicted within 1–2% error. o Advanced European aggregators run their own weather models, blending meteorology with machine learning. o Japan lags ~10 years behind Europe but expected to catch up by 2030. • Battery Storage o Still small-scale in Japan, so electricity treated as non-storable. o As storage grows, optimization will become more complex and profitable.  Data Science & Engineering at Enexia • Data Science Team o Builds generation and price forecasting models. o Runs optimization to select positions maximizing P&L. o Constant R&D on new features, especially for difficult price prediction. • Engineering Team o Maintains 24/7 operational systems for forecast submission and market transactions. o Deploys DS models via MLOps for scalability and automation. • Collaboration: business depends on both teams—DS builds logic, Engineering operationalizes it. • Tools: Google Cloud, Python, VS Code, Jupyter, Airflow (Composer), Git. • Pain point: DS code often not deployment-ready; engineers rewrite for production. • Highly valuable: people who can bridge DS ↔ engineering.

Business Scope & Risk Management • Core: renewable aggregation (forecasting, imbalance management, trading). • Also: wholesale electricity (with derivative-like terms), retail license, risk management. • Risk management: o Imbalance risk is not conventionally hedgeable (unknown until the moment). o Managed statistically across many 30-minute intervals. o Considered a competitive advantage rather than a risk to offload. • Tech: not commercialized externally—engineers focus on internal scalability.  Strategic Goals • Near-term (2–3 years): o Rapidly accumulate renewable assets under management. o Achieve #1 aggregator position in Japan. o Asset diversification across regions → stability + volume discounts. • Longer-term: o After Japan, expand into Europe (advanced) or Southeast Asia (once markets liberalize). o Potential to enter as both advisor and market participant.
